# Changelog

## [0.2.0] - 2026-03-30

### Added

- A deeper research base across Stanford, NBER, MIT, WEF, and Anthropic sources
- New evidence on adoption speed, early-career labor-market pressure, task exposure, and worker preferences around human agency
- A dedicated section explaining "directed systems" near the front of the guide
- A task-level work audit section to help readers evaluate exposure and leverage inside their own roles
- Operating rules for directed systems: keep humans close to consequence, preserve explanation paths, protect skill repetition, shorten feedback loops, and respect worker-preferred agency
- An explicit disclosure note about AI-assisted research and drafting with Claude Code and OpenAI Codex
- Paper-like structure in `GUIDE.md`, including abstract, argument sections, explicit limitations, references, and an author's note
- A more explicit distinction between practitioner analysis and peer-reviewed academic research
- A diagnostic framing for directed systems based on shifted initiative, retained human consequence, and asymmetric learning

### Changed

- Reframed the opening thesis from general AI disruption to a more specific shift: machines increasingly direct work while humans retain execution and responsibility
- Strengthened the guide with recent research on real-world adoption, augmentation versus automation, and early employment effects
- Tightened the "Why Humans Are Still Necessary" section around judgment, adaptation, trust, and consequence management
- Reworked "How to Prepare" into six clearer moves: audit tasks, move toward judgment and exceptions, learn verification, combine domain depth with AI literacy, preserve core skills, and treat early-career leverage as a real design problem
- Reframed the document from a practical guide into an industry position paper while keeping the same March release line
- Tightened the tone to reduce pedantry, inflated novelty, pseudo-academic signaling, and generic AI-written phrasing
- Reorganized the argument around adoption, exposure, human agency, labor-market effects, misdesign risk, and implications for workers, managers, and builders
- Deepened the paper's contribution by relating the framework more explicitly to prior automation literature, objections, and case sketches
- Updated project metadata from `v0.1` / January 2026 to `v0.2.0` / March 2026

### Removed

- Broad or weakly supported claims about AI limitations that did not help the reader act
- The "prompt engineering" detour in favor of a more durable emphasis on verification, human agency, and system design
- The thinner bibliography that left several current claims under-cited
- Guide-oriented framing that made the document read more like advice than argument
